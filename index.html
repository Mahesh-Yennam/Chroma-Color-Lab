🔬 Subtractive vs. Additive Chromatic Physics

Why does mixing Yellow and Blue paint produce vivid Green, but mixing Yellow and Blue light beams produces White? Explore the fundamental difference between light wavelength absorption and light emission.

🎨 Physical Art & Print

Subtractive Mixing (Pigment Chemistry)

Pigments work by absorbing (subtracting) specific light wavelengths from ambient white light, reflecting only the unabsorbed remainder to your eyes.

  • Primary Pigments: Cyan, Magenta, Yellow (or Red, Yellow, Blue in classical art)
  • Key Formula: More colors mixed = Less reflected light = Darker result
  • Ultimate Blend: Mixing all pigments absorbs all light → Black
💡 Photonics & Screens

Additive Mixing (Optical Light Synthesis)

Optical light synthesis occurs when emitted photons of different wavelengths combine in the eye's photoreceptor cone cells (L, M, S cones).

  • Primary Emitters: Red (700nm), Green (546nm), Blue (435nm)
  • Key Formula: More light beams added = More photon energy = Brighter result
  • Ultimate Blend: Mixing all visible wavelengths triggers all cones → Pure White

⚡ The Classic Mixing Paradox

Color Combination Subtractive (Paint Pigment) Additive (Stage Light) Why They Differ
Blue + Yellow Green White / Gray Yellow reflects red+green; blue reflects blue+green. Overlap = green reflected in paint. In light, red+green+blue triggers all cones to see white.
Red + Green Muddy Olive Brown Yellow Pigments absorb both red and green wavelengths. In light, stimulating both L (red) and M (green) cones produces pure yellow perception.
Red + Blue Deep Violet Magenta In optics, non-spectral magenta is synthesized by combining opposite ends of the visible spectrum.
